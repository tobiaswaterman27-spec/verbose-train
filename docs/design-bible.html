The Fellaron Register

What has actually been decided, and nothing that has not. The story and the cast are settled. The hundred is planned down to how it divides — by element, by line length and by what the bodies are made of — but the entries themselves get written a line at a time, and only five exist so far.

III

The story

Fen opened the vault himself. The Gatekeeper is genuinely dangerous. Nobody tells you either of those things — you find them out.

1 · Sorting day

You arrive at Hollowbrook, dorm next to Wren, meet Cobb mid-complaint about paperwork. Ceremony happens. You pick a starter; Wren picks next. Cobb is told he's "on the list, technically" and handed the third with no ceremony at all.

2 · That night

Alarms. The vault under the school — empty. Morning announcement: a break-in, term suspended, nobody leaves the grounds "for their own safety." Fen looks exhausted in a way that reads as fear to the staff and as something else to Wren.

3 · The hole in the story

Bix mentions, without emphasis, that the vault door locks from the inside. Nobody else seems to notice he's said this.

4 · You leave anyway

Not to solve a mystery yet — the lockdown is worse than whatever's out there. Over the fence at night, badly.

5 · The regions

Each one shows damage first: fellarons mutated wrong and aggressive, ground torn up, weather gone strange. You clear the immediate mess before anyone local will talk to you. Bix's old notes, left at outposts decades ago, keep turning up exactly when needed.

6 · Fen keeps almost catching up

Not to stop you — to beat you there. Every region you clear, his own attempt to quietly recapture the Gatekeeper has already gone wrong nearby, and made it worse.

7 · The proof

Around the halfway point: an early draft of the announcement Fen gave the council, written and dated before the vault ever opened. The crisis was planned.

8 · Confronting him

He doesn't get chased off. He goes quiet, and stops getting in your way — which is worse, from his side, than being stopped. He already knows he's lost and is just watching it run past the point he can fix it.

9 · Escalation

Knowing the adults can't be trusted to handle this, you move faster through the last regions. The Gatekeeper isn't fleeing — it's aiming, back toward wherever it was first found before it was ever sealed.

10 · The confrontation

Not won by hitting harder — Fen already tried that twice and made it worse both times. It stops when it's worn down to nothing left to prove, and in that moment it reads as grief, not violence.

11 · What it actually wanted

Not freedom, not revenge — centuries in the dark, blamed for what it does to anything nearby, never once asked if it wanted to be there.

12 · The choice

Three ways this can go, and the game does not say which is right: reseal it, safe and final; let it go, into the wildest region, unwatched and unbound; or give it ground — one unpopulated region, watched but not caged, a compromise nobody's fully comfortable with.

13 · Fen, after

Ashworth sees exactly what the "managed incident" cost. Fen isn't thrown out — quietly moved off headmaster duties, teaching something small, and everyone is a little too polite to bring it up.

14 · Denouement

Term restarts. Cobb's notebook gets bound and shelved in the school library without his permission. Wren is already planning the next trip.

IV

The ten elements

Now committed. The wheel was inherited from an earlier draft and carried along unexamined for a while. The roster allocation below is built on it — ten Fellarons to each of these ten — so it is load-bearing now and changing it would mean redividing the hundred. The regions are still only names.

What makes a Fellaron that element

The first roster failed partly because the element arrived as a colour — a green one, a red one — which is a decision you can make after the fact and therefore not a decision at all. So each element gets a rule about geometry instead. It has to be answerable by pointing at the silhouette. If the only Verdant thing about a Fellaron is that it is green, it is not Verdant, and it does not go on the register.

V

The hundred

The standard

The first attempt at this roster produced eight Fellarons in an afternoon and seven were thrown away. They failed for the same three reasons every time: the body plan was one already on the roster, the element showed up as a colour instead of as a shape, and there was no answer to what the animal did when nobody was looking.

Nothing joins the register until all six of these are written down, in words, before any geometry is placed.

  1. A body plan no other Fellaron uses. Not a variation. A different animal.
  2. A living. What it eats, where it sleeps, what it is afraid of.
  3. Its element in its shape. Colour is the last five per cent, never the first.
  4. Three things you could learn by watching it. If there is no answer, it is not finished.
  5. A reason it changes that is the thing it already does.
  6. One fact with no gameplay purpose whatsoever.

And one rule about process: a line at a time. The seven that were cut were designed as a flat batch of eight unrelated animals, and a batch like that converges — the second borrows from the first, and the eighth is a recolour of the third. A line does not have that problem, because the second form is supposed to answer the first. So the unit of work is one whole line, one to three forms, start to finish, and nothing else is opened until it is done.

How the hundred divides

Ten to an element — but not ten the same way twice, or the roster reads as a spreadsheet. Each element gets its own mix of line lengths, so some elements are mostly long families and others are mostly one-offs that never change at all.

What they are made of

Left to itself, every entry on a roster like this becomes a mammal with a gimmick. So the body-plan split is fixed before anything is designed, and a category that is full is full — if the vertebrate count is spent, the next idea has to be a mechanism or a rock or nothing.

Two thirds of the roster is not an animal.
