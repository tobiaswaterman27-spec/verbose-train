Wildhollow · design bible · draft one

The Wildhollow Register

A valley, a school, ten elements and one hundred Kith. Everything the game needs to be written down before it can be built.

I

Premise

Mutation is supposed to take a lifetime. In Wildhollow it has started taking a season.

The valley sits over ten Wellsprings — places where a single element runs close enough to the surface to touch. The creatures of the valley, called Kith, change slowly and in step with the Wellspring nearest where they were born. That rhythm has held for as long as anyone has kept records.

Three years ago it began to accelerate.

You arrive at Hollowbrook Academy as a first-year Binder, in a class of ten. Each of you is entrusted with a Kith drawn from a different Wellspring line. The work is ordinary at first — catalogue the valley, learn the ten elements, keep your Kith alive through winter.

Then your class finds the Verdant Wellspring drained to grey mud, and the things living around it wearing shapes the register has no names for.

II

The three acts

Act I — Enrolment

Ten students, ten Wellsprings

Choose your Kith and meet the nine classmates who take the others. Learn the element wheel through field practicals in Thistlemoor and Duskwood — the tutorial is a syllabus, so teaching never has to stop the story. The act closes on the drained Verdant Wellspring and the first unrecorded mutation.

Act II — The Survey

One region per element, one arc per classmate

The class is sent to survey all ten Wellsprings. Each is a region, a dungeon and a classmate's story at once, so the roster and the cast advance on the same track. You learn the Wellsprings are not failing — they are being tapped. Ives Kalder vanishes for a week and returns with a Kith three mutations ahead of schedule and no account of how.

Act III — Forced Growth

The cost of not waiting

Headmaster Vosk has been draining the Wellsprings to induce mutation on demand. He is not cruel — he lost a Kith to a change that arrived too late to save it, and has spent twenty years making certain no student of his ever waits again. The ending asks one question: reseal the Wellsprings and lose every forced mutation in the valley, your classmates' Kith among them — or leave them open and accept a valley that never stops changing.

Theme. Forced growth against growth in its own time. Vosk is what happens when a teacher decides a student cannot afford to develop at their own pace — which makes him wrong in a way the player can recognise from the inside, rather than a villain to be defeated.

III

The ten elements

Each element has a Wellspring, and each Wellspring is a region. The map is the type chart — learning where things live is learning what beats what.

IV

Hollowbrook Academy

Faculty

The class of ten

You take one of these ten lines. Whichever you choose, that classmate takes the next line over — so the cohort always covers all ten elements, and your pick changes who your natural rival is.

V

The starter lines

Ten lines, three forms each. Starters and their mutations hold a single element for all three forms — it is what marks a Wellspring line apart from wild Kith, who may carry two.
